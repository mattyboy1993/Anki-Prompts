For a documentary card, what should the question specify?	The specific claim or event and the source segment or speaker.
How should you create cards from articles or essays?	Ask about one claim or finding tied to a clear section or argument.
What is a good way to test comparisons across sources?	Ask for the specific difference and name both sources in the question.
How should you handle recurring concepts across media?	Create separate cards per source and disambiguate with titles.
What is a good way to test a timeline in a series?	Ask about one dated event and its position relative to another event.
How should you handle opinion-based content?	Ask for the stated opinion and who expressed it, not whether it is true.
What should a card about a creator’s intent include?	The creator name, the work, and the specific intent or message described.
How should you test a cause-and-effect claim in any medium?	Ask for the cause and the exact effect as stated in the source.
What is the best way to handle names, places, or jargon?	Ask for one definition or identification with enough context to disambiguate.
How should you handle unfamiliar acronyms?	Ask for the acronym expansion and what it refers to in context.
What should you do when a prompt feels too broad?	Split it into smaller cards until each tests one precise fact.
How should you ensure cards are durable over time?	Use stable identifiers like titles, dates, or named entities in the question.
