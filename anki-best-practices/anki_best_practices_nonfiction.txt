For a nonfiction concept card, what should the question include?	The exact term and the context or domain it belongs to.
How should you test a nonfiction argument or thesis?	Ask for the thesis in one sentence or the key claim the author defends.
What is the best way to handle frameworks or models?	Create separate cards for each component and how it functions.
How should you ask about evidence used in a nonfiction book?	Ask for the specific piece of evidence and what claim it supports.
When should you use examples in nonfiction cards?	When they clarify an abstract idea or differentiate similar concepts.
How can you keep definitions precise?	Use one-sentence definitions with necessary qualifiers only.
What should you do with multi-step processes?	Turn each step into a separate card and keep the order explicit.
How do you avoid overly broad prompts about a chapter?	Ask about one clear claim or finding from that chapter.
What is a good way to handle statistics or numbers?	Ask for the number and what it measures in a specific context.
How should you test comparisons between two ideas?	Ask for the distinguishing feature and name both ideas in the question.
What should a "why" question about a nonfiction concept target?	The causal explanation the author gives, not a general opinion.
How should you handle author recommendations?	Create a card for each recommendation and its stated rationale.
